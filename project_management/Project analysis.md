# Choices and Decisions

<span style='color: grey'>

## ➡️ Graphical library
</span>

We chose to use Raylib as the graphical library for this project.

However, as stated in the subject, we had to limit our use of the library to what the MLX is capable of  
doing. Certain actions that we could have performed simply by using the library's functions therefore  
had to be broken down into more precise, smaller actions compatible with the MLX.

<span style='color: grey'>

## ➡️ Textures
</span>

Our Pacman does not have any texture in its assets. All needed textures are generated by our program,  
during execution. This allows us to have a program that is smaller to download.

<span style='color: grey'>

## ➡️ Bonuses
</span>

The subject does not mention any bonuses, but we chose to implement a fair amount of them. That goes  
from extra objects to help the player during the game, to — why not, after all — full-blown bonus games.  
And as orobert said :  

<i>
"It's just in case they add bonus features right before we hand in the project. Might as well be prepared for it."
</i>

<span style='color: grey'>

## ➡️ Documentation
</span>

Although not required by the subject, we chose to write the "project management" section in English for  
the sake of consistency with the rest of the project.

Since the code, the README, and even the game itself are available in English, writing this part in French  
would not have made sense.

<span style='color: grey'>

## ➡️ Team organization
</span>

The project was already partially underway when lbonnet joined the team. Some parts were therefore  
already done, and others not at all, even though they should have been taken into consideration from  
the beginning. The distribution of tasks, apart from the work done collectively, thus took place almost  
automatically:

- orobert: graphical visualization, maze adaptation, mini-games and game lore.
- lbonnet: project management, construction of the executable, tests and bug hunting.

Ideas, improvements, and proposals were shared throughout the development of the program, including  
with other students recruited for testing.

Since orobert is not present many days of the week, each person worked on their own part independently.  
The work was gradually consolidated, thanks to GitHub and occasional in-person meetings. Consequently,  
on-site testing —including testing with other students— was primarily carried out by lbonnet. This did not  
in any way hinder the team's workflow, which proceeded smoothly; in fact, it even helped uncover hidden  
bugs.
